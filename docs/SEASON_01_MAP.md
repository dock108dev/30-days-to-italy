# Rehearsal Content Map — Coastal Italy

**Implementation note (Prompt 8A.1):** This map is the content authority for 30 **pre-departure preparation sessions**, not 30 vacation days. The web prototype has stable IDs `day-00` through `day-30`. Day 0 is immediately available; Day 1 is scheduled 30 days before departure through Day 30 one day before departure. Missed unlocked sessions remain available. Arrival, Days 1–7, Day 13, and Day 21 are implemented as independent registered modules; the remaining 21 slots carry truthful planning metadata and cannot be played. Current game state uses episode IDs only, and Pocket Deck evidence records observed responses rather than intended episode targets.

## Rehearsal premise

The player is preparing for a 7–10 day solo vacation in Campania and along the Amalfi Coast. The map’s fictional **Hotel Sirena**, **Marina di Lume**, and recurring characters provide a coherent rehearsal world in which high-value situations can recur and become more difficult before departure.

Real geography supplies orientation and recognizable travel decisions. Businesses, residences, recurring characters, prices, schedules, disruptions, and story events are fictional so the season remains coherent when real-world details change.

The map is a content source, not the final countdown schedule or a set of exhaustive scripts. Each row distinguishes the practical player objective from hidden language work. A future scheduling pass may combine, reorder, or omit rows based on departure timing and trip relevance.

## Recurring language threads

| Thread | Early use | Friction use | Later use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Request and quantity | Room, coffee, chair, food | Ask for replacement or alternative | Make a compact familiar order or a more precise request |
| Refuse and limit | `Solo questo`, `No, grazie` | Reject an extra charge or inadequate fix | Decline an invitation or optional conversation warmly |
| Clarify and confirm | Ask to repeat a time or price | Distinguish route, item, promise, or changed time | Confirm a multi-step plan without transcript-first support |
| Explain a problem | Name a missing or broken thing | Contrast expected and actual result | Summarize what happened and whether it was resolved |
| Prior events and commitments | Simple `ieri` references begin around Day 10 | `Aveva detto…`, `Ho già…` enforce promises | Tell a short account and close the loop with familiar characters |
| Preference and alternatives | Choose card/cash, bus/ferry, this/that | Find another item, route, or repair | Compare what worked and state plans or uncertainty |
| Exit and boundary | Thank, pay, leave | Stop circular repair or postpone discussion | Accept, decline, or defer familiarity on the player’s terms |

## Support progression

- **Day 0:** all supports prominent; the system samples audio-only, replay, transcript, mixed-language response, clarification, and exit.
- **Days 1–5:** replay, slow replay, transcript, and an optional phrase cue are obvious.
- **Days 6–10:** the same supports remain available, but phrase cues recede and first presentations add light ambient context.
- **Days 11–15:** complications require listening for a changed detail; replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript remain available while their controls become less prominent.
- **Days 16–20:** prior-event and contrast language recurs; replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript remain available, while proactive cues are limited to recovery after difficulty.
- **Days 21–25:** familiar voices carry more natural reductions; the same listening supports remain available, and optional social lines are never prerequisite to the task.
- **Days 26–30:** known structures combine in short, denser exchanges; replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript remain requestable, and accessibility overrides the planned visual fade.

Support use affects later presentation and competence evidence, not whether a practical outcome counts.

In the planning matrices below, the shorthand `replay/transcript` always includes requestable careful-speed replay. Later days reduce control prominence, not availability.

## State dependencies and callbacks

- The apartment repair arc uses explicit commitments from Days 11, 17, 20, and 27. If hot water is fixed early, later scenes shift from “still broken” to checking repair quality, a different apartment inconvenience, or confirming promised compensation; they never pretend failure continued.
- Giulia’s Day 21 question references the ferry only if the Day 19 cancellation occurred in the canonical path. Otherwise she asks about an authored recent event she could plausibly know, or simply offers the usual order.
- “The usual” is available only after the player ordered the same item enough times and did not object to it being remembered. The player can correct or disable the assumption.
- Day 23’s parcel can follow a successful Day 16 collection, a redelivery, or a neighbor acceptance path. Marta never possesses it without a validated handoff.
- The Day 25 event is optional. Day 29 may mention attendance, declining, or nothing, but cannot invent participation.
- Day 28 adapts to prior transport choices. It raises complexity through transfer or changed timing while preserving at least one achievable route or a legitimate decision to postpone.
- Day 30 reconciles every open commitment. It does not convert unresolved costs or promises into an automatically happy ending.

## Plausible player paths

### Quiet independent path

The player uses minimal fragments, refuses extras, rarely accepts optional openings, and still builds strong evidence through correct practical action. Characters become efficient and respectful: Giulia may ask “the usual?” but does not demand a life story. The ending emphasizes control and boundaries, not number of friends.

### Supported listener path

The player frequently reveals transcripts early, then gradually acts after one or two replays in recurring contexts. Outcomes remain valid. The closing reflection highlights the shift in listening support and successful repairs rather than presenting transcript use as failure.

### Warm familiar path

The player sometimes asks one follow-up, accepts the local event, and tells Giulia how the ferry problem ended. This creates more callbacks and optional dialogue, but not superior rewards or easier core services.

### Friction-and-recovery path

The player accepts a wrong item, misses a changed stop, or leaves the hot-water promise vague. Later episodes provide authored recovery: spending extra money, trying again, citing the promise, or choosing another route. The season stays playable and remembers the consequence.

Paths can mix. They are not classes selected at the beginning, and relationship tone must not become a covert score.

## Representative episode 1: Hotel arrival and obtaining the key (Day 0)

### Player-facing objective

Check in, obtain the key, understand how to reach the room, and go to bed. Breakfast information is useful but does not need to be tested before the player can leave.

### Entry state and scene

- Location: Hotel Sirena, Salerno, late evening.
- NPC: Elena, a calm night clerk who wants to verify the booking and complete check-in.
- Known state: reservation surname, two-night stay, room assigned but key not issued.
- Player context: visibly tired; no prior competence assumptions.

### Hidden design targets

- Extract a greeting/check-in gist from audio.
- Identify with a name or booking fragment.
- Understand or clarify room number and floor.
- Demonstrate that a short answer and polite exit work.
- Sample replay, slow replay, transcript, mixed language, Spanish crossover, and one repair without assigning a proficiency label.

### Listening design

Elena begins with one short natural line asking the name on the reservation. After validation, she gives the room number and a simple direction. Breakfast closing time is a separate optional informational turn so the player is not asked to retain three essential facts at once.

Complication: a low-confidence surname match can require one clarification or spelling choice. It cannot trigger unrelated questions about the player’s trip.

### Encounter beats and bounded intents

1. **Arrival:** greet, state check-in intent, or show uncertainty.
2. **Identity:** provide surname/booking; ask Elena to repeat; or offer booking evidence.
3. **Key handoff:** once identity is validated, the game applies `hotel_key_issued` and room facts.
4. **Essential direction:** player may confirm the floor or accept it.
5. **Optional information:** breakfast time and one optional welcome question.
6. **Exit:** thanks, tiredness, defer conversation, or simply leave after acknowledgment.

Recognized intent is not enough to issue the key: the authoritative rule also requires a matching reservation or an authored staff-lookup outcome.

### Support

Unlimited replay, slower replay, transcript reveal, and a compact intent cue such as “give the name / ask to repeat / show booking.” English nouns and incomplete Italian are accepted. English meaning appears only as late recovery or post-resolution reflection.

### Outcomes and consequences

- **Direct success:** key issued; room and access understood.
- **Supported success:** same world outcome, with support evidence recorded.
- **Delayed success:** Elena verifies the booking manually; time advances modestly.
- **Partial:** key issued but breakfast time remains unknown.
- **Abandoned:** player waits in the lobby or requests English assistance; recovery remains available.

The scene never gates the key on answering `Perché è in Italia?` or similar pedagogical prompts.

### Remembered later

Support preference, demonstrated moves, room/key state, breakfast fact if understood, and whether the player prefers minimal interaction. Raw speculative biography is not stored.

### Authoring/evaluation cases

Accept `Sono Michael`, surname only, `Ho reservation`, and plausible Spanish-influenced check-in attempts when the booking can be resolved. Require repair for a wrong surname or ambiguous room confirmation. Verify that `Sono stanco. Domani?`, `Più tardi`, and thanks-plus-exit end the optional conversation immediately.

## Representative episode 2: Renting beach equipment (Day 4)

### Player-facing objective

Rent one beach chair and one umbrella for the day without buying an unwanted package.

### Entry state and scene

- Location: Lido Piccola Luna kiosk.
- NPC: Nadia, brisk and practical; this is the first canonical meeting.
- Known state: current time, player money, equipment availability, authored day price, and closing time.
- Constraint: the player is alone, while the common offer is a two-chair package.

### Hidden design targets

Request, quantity, duration, understand a bundled offer, refuse an extra, clarify price, and end the transaction. Recur `vorrei`, `solo`, numbers, card payment, and `no, grazie` from earlier scenes.

### Listening design

Nadia asks whether the player wants the standard two-chair package and states its price. The key discrimination is quantity, not perfect transcription. After selection, she states the equipment return/closing time in a separate turn.

Complication: Nadia initially assumes two chairs. This is a believable sales default, not a deliberate language trap.

### Encounter constraints and flexible input

The interpreter may propose intents such as request-equipment, set-quantity, ask-price, accept-package, refuse-extra, pay-card, or leave. Essential slots are equipment, quantity, and duration. `Un chair e un umbrella, per oggi` can fill them with mixed-language nouns; `Sì` before quantity is resolved cannot.

The rule layer checks availability and price and offers only these authored outcomes: one-chair custom rental, standard package, chair-only rental, or no rental. The NPC cannot invent a discount or equipment category.

### Support

Replay, slower playback, transcript, item illustrations after the first listen, and an optional move cue. A revealed price card must not replace the need to hear the later closing time, though the player can reveal that transcript too.

### Valid endings

Take the chosen equipment, decline the whole offer and leave, pay and say thanks, or ask one final time clarification. Nadia does not ask why the player is alone.

### Outcomes and consequences

- Correct minimal rental at authored price.
- More expensive standard package accepted.
- Chair-only compromise.
- No rental, preserving money and time.
- Misunderstood quantity noticed before charge and repaired.

Applied state includes money, rental inventory, return obligation, closing fact, and Nadia’s memory of the actual package. A charge is never applied on low-confidence quantity interpretation.

### Authoring/evaluation cases

Test terse Italian (`Una sola, grazie`), mixed nouns, Spanish `sombrilla`, contradictory numbers, acceptance followed by correction, refusal, insufficient money, and an attempted invented bargain. Verify that consequence descriptions match the charged package exactly.

## Representative episode 3: Wrong and overcharged café order (Day 13)

### Player-facing objective

Resolve receiving the wrong drink and an extra line item on the bill. The player decides whether they want a remake, a refund/adjustment, or to accept a bounded compromise.

### Entry state and scene

- Location: Bar Gabbiano during a moderately busy period.
- NPC: Giulia, who recognizes the player if prior visits established that state.
- Canonical facts: what was ordered, what arrived, receipt lines, prices, payment status, time available, and any remembered preference.
- The error is authored and real; the system must not make the player argue against an uncertain reality.

### Hidden design targets

Explain a problem, contrast expected and received, correct a misunderstanding, cite receipt evidence, request a remedy, accept/refuse a proposal, and preserve or limit relationship warmth. Recur ordering, this/that, negation, price, and card language.

### Listening design

Giulia’s first repair response contains an apology and a proposed remedy. The player needs to catch whether she proposes replacing only the drink, removing only the extra line, or doing both. Café noise is present but the line remains intelligible.

Complication: the drink error and extra charge are distinct. Fixing one does not silently fix the other.

### Encounter beats

1. Player notices or accepts the mismatched drink.
2. Player notices or accepts the receipt line.
3. A complaint can name either or both issues in any order.
4. Giulia acknowledges what she understood and proposes one authored remedy.
5. Player accepts, clarifies the remaining issue, requests another allowed remedy, or leaves.
6. The rule layer applies exact refund, remake, inventory, time, and relationship consequences.

The encounter may take multiple cycles because two consequential details need resolution. Giulia still asks only necessary questions and never turns the dispute into a grammar exercise.

### Allowed outcome set

- Both errors fixed: correct drink plus removed charge.
- Financial correction only; player keeps or declines the wrong drink.
- Drink remade but charge still present, creating a partial outcome or another repair chance.
- Player knowingly accepts the mismatch for a small authored adjustment.
- Player pays and leaves, retaining a valid unresolved-service memory.
- Escalation to a manager if Giulia cannot authorize the selected remedy.

The model cannot award free future drinks, arbitrary compensation, or relationship reconciliation outside these rules.

### Support and correction

Replay, careful-speed replay, transcript, visible receipt, and a late recovery cue separating “wrong item” from “extra charge.” Feedback prioritizes language that would change the remedy, such as `Avevo ordinato…` or `C'è anche questo sul conto`, and ignores low-value errors.

### Valid endings and remembered state

Accept the remedy, say the issue remains, ask to discuss it after the rush, pay the corrected amount, or leave. Store the exact financial resolution, item resolution, time cost, a qualitative trust/irritation descriptor, and a compact authored memory that Giulia can later reference. Do not store a fabricated apology or promise.

### Authoring/evaluation cases

Test complaints that name only one issue, confuse refund with remake, contain Spanish forms, use only pointing language, become firm but not abusive, or explicitly waive the problem. Confirm cents-exact reconciliation and that later callbacks reflect the actual remedy.

## Representative episode 4: Familiar bartender, optional further conversation (Day 21)

### Player-facing objective

Order or pay for a drink. Giulia may ask how the ferry disruption ended; answering is optional.

### Entry state and scene

- Location: Bar Gabbiano at a quieter hour.
- NPC: Giulia, with recognition and tone derived from validated prior encounters.
- Canonical facts: usual order only if established; Day 13 resolution; Day 19 ferry outcome; player conversation-boundary pattern.
- If those facts are absent, neutral authored variants replace the callback rather than inventing history.

### Hidden design targets

Efficient familiar transaction, understand a natural callback, give a short account of a past event, defer, end warmly, or ask one optional follow-up. This is the first clear demonstration that conversational growth means more available choices, not mandatory talk.

### Listening design

Giulia uses a familiar natural pace: `Alla fine, com'è andata con il traghetto?` The first-listen challenge is recognizing that she is asking about the outcome, not reopening the ticket transaction. The transactional payment turn remains separate.

### Interaction branches

- **Minimal:** `Bene, grazie. Con la carta.` Giulia processes payment and lets the player leave.
- **Boundary:** `Te lo racconto un'altra volta` or `Ora devo andare.` Giulia accepts it without relationship penalty.
- **Brief account:** player states cancelled/rebooked/bus/abandoned outcome; system checks it against canonical history before allowing it into character memory.
- **Conversational:** player adds an opinion or asks Giulia one question; Giulia answers within authored personal facts, then offers a natural close.
- **Correction of familiarity:** player rejects an assumed usual order or says they do not want to discuss the trip; both are respected.

No branch gives better service, points, or privileged access solely for sociability.

### State and model boundary

The interpreter extracts transaction intent, desired drink, payment method, conversation choice, and claimed ferry facts. The rule layer validates price and product. A claimed past event that conflicts with world state may be treated as misunderstanding, joke, or correction opportunity according to authored policy, but it cannot rewrite Day 19.

### Support

Replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript are always available. No proactive phrase card appears for the optional question; one can be requested. If the player repeatedly misses that the social turn is optional, the interface may explicitly label “You can answer, defer, or pay and leave.”

### Outcomes and remembered state

The drink/payment outcome is independent from the social choice. Character memory may add the validated trip result, a stated preference, or “prefers to talk another time.” Relationship tone can become warmer, remain efficient, or recover after Day 13; none is ranked as the win state.

### Authoring/evaluation cases

Verify minimal answers, direct exits, Spanish-shaped past accounts, a false recollection by Giulia, a player claim inconsistent with canonical state, a corrected usual order, follow-up questions outside Giulia’s authored knowledge, and latency that might make silence feel like pressure. Every optional branch must return to a clean exit within one additional turn unless the player explicitly continues.

## Season-level content checks

Before production, validate that:

- every required objective has a terse successful path;
- every optional social opening has decline, defer, and leave paths;
- each high-value move recurs in a near, friction, and spaced context;
- later callbacks have both present- and absent-state variants;
- no real schedule, business, price, or operating claim is required for plot integrity;
- listening difficulty rises deliberately rather than simply making audio faster;
- every consequential ambiguity has a clarification threshold;
- failures preserve a coherent recovery or alternate path; and
- the Day 30 reflection can cite concrete outcomes and support changes without producing a score.
