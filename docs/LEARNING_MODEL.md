# Learning Model

## Purpose

The learning system activates practical Italian inside guided pre-departure rehearsal. It does not expose a grammar syllabus as the traveler’s task. It chooses likely trip situations, listening demands, recurrences, and concise feedback so that previously recognizable language becomes available under real-time pressure and the most useful material can enter the Pocket Deck.

This is an initial model for one known learner, not a claim about all learners or a formal proficiency assessment.

The day bands below describe preparation sequence and difficulty, not literal vacation days. Trip Mode does not continue this learning progression; it provides fast assistance without lessons or correction.

## Language-state model

Language is tracked as evidence about **communicative moves in context**, not as a single mastery percentage.

| State | Working definition | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| Dormant | Previously studied; may feel familiar after exposure but is not retrieved spontaneously | Use a meaningful prompt, allow recognition support, then revisit soon in a changed context |
| Recognizable | Understood in text or slow/clear audio but not reliably at realistic speed or available for use | Withhold transcript first, repeat across speakers and acoustics, invite a short response |
| Active | Retrieved or understood with useful accuracy in more than one context, with manageable support | Space recurrence, vary tense/politeness/detail, and combine with another move |
| New | No reliable evidence of prior knowledge | Model inside an immediately useful situation; do not demand unsupported production |

The state attaches to a move plus relevant conditions: for example, “clarify a price in writing” is not the same evidence as “recognize a corrected price in fast audio.” A player can be active with `vorrei` requests but only recognizable with past-event explanations.

State transitions are provisional inferences. One lucky success does not establish active control, and one tired failure does not erase it.

## Communicative-move progression

The season uses three overlapping bands:

### Days 1–10: transact and leave

Request, identify, confirm, refuse, choose, state quantity/time, pay, ask for repetition, and end politely. The player learns that fragments can work and that leaving is allowed.

### Days 11–20: handle friction and changed circumstances

Explain a present problem, distinguish expected from received, correct a misunderstanding, refer to a prior event or promise, ask for an alternative, reject an inadequate response, compare options, and change a plan.

### Days 21–30: optional familiarity and conversational openings

Give a short account, express an opinion or uncertainty, connect past and future, ask one follow-up, accept or decline an invitation, set a boundary warmly, and close a familiar conversation.

Later bands continue to require earlier moves. A complicated ferry change still needs a destination, time, preference, clarification, payment decision, and exit.

## Structure activation and recurrence

Each target moves through a deliberate recurrence cycle:

1. **Comprehensible encounter:** the form appears in a context that makes the function inferable.
2. **Supported use:** the player can reuse it with transcript, phrase cue, or mixed-language response.
3. **Near recurrence:** it returns within roughly two or three episodes with a different noun or speaker.
4. **Friction recurrence:** the move is needed when a detail changes, an assumption fails, or the NPC misunderstands.
5. **Spaced recurrence:** it reappears after several days in combination with another move.
6. **Independent option:** the player can choose it without the objective naming the language function.

The cadence is a content-planning rule, not a rigid algorithm. High-value survival moves recur more often. No target is considered robust until it has evidence across at least two contexts and more than one listening condition.

Core recurring frames include:

- `Vorrei…`, `Mi serve…`, `Posso avere…?` for requests;
- `Solo…`, `Non mi serve`, `No, grazie` for limits and refusal;
- `Ha detto…?`, `Può ripetere?`, `Più piano, per favore` for listening repair;
- `Preferisco…`, `Meglio…`, `Invece…` for preference and alternatives;
- `C'è un problema con…`, `Manca…`, `Non funziona…` for problems;
- `Aveva detto…`, `Ho già…`, `Ieri…` for prior facts and commitments;
- `Non questo, quello…`, `Intendevo…` for correction; and
- `Devo andare`, `Ne parliamo dopo`, `Un'altra volta` for exits and boundaries.

These are authoring resources, not required exact answers.

## Listening-support progression

Listening difficulty changes on controlled dimensions: line length, speech rate, speaker familiarity, accent/coloring, reductions, acoustic setting, information density, and predictability. Authors should increase one or two dimensions at a time.

| Stage | Default first presentation | Available support | Intended evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Clear, short line; no transcript | Unlimited replay, slower replay, transcript, post-resolution meaning | Separate reading, listening, and response baselines |
| Days 1–5 | Clear natural speech, predictable context | Unlimited replay, slow replay, transcript, optional phrase cue | Act on a key detail without reading first |
| Days 6–10 | Natural speech with light ambient context | Replay, slow replay, transcript; cues less prominent | Recognize recurrent transaction frames across speakers |
| Days 11–15 | Longer or corrective turn; one changed detail | Replay, careful-speed replay, transcript; controls become less prominent | Detect the problem rather than merely the topic |
| Days 16–20 | Natural repair, contrast, or prior-event reference | Replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript remain available | Follow a changed plan and verify essential details |
| Days 21–25 | Familiar speakers at natural pace, optional social opener | Replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript on demand | Understand the opener without being forced to answer socially |
| Days 26–30 | Mixed known structures, denser but still short turns | Replay, careful-speed replay, and transcript remain available; minimal proactive cues | Complete multi-move objectives with decreasing reliance |

Support never disappears globally. Careful-speed replay remains available throughout all 30 days, even when its control becomes less prominent. Accessibility needs, fatigue, novel content, and high-consequence decisions also justify support. The system fades **prompts and default prominence**, not the player’s right to ask for help.

## English fallback as a teaching transition

When the player reaches for an English frame such as “I need,” “I would like,” or “I am,” the system should not treat the response as either a failed conversation turn or an immediately successful English transaction. It briefly pauses the scene and teaches one reusable Italian building block in the current context.

The teaching transition:

1. preserves the current NPC turn and makes no world-state mutation;
2. acknowledges the English meaning without judgment;
3. shows one compact pattern, one explanation, and one scene-specific example;
4. lets the player insert that example or rebuild it independently;
5. returns to the same conversation turn for actual use; and
6. keeps the same high-frequency patterns available in a permanent refresher toolkit.

The initial toolkit covers `Mi serve / Mi servono`, `Vorrei`, `Sono`, `Ho`, `Devo`, `Posso / Può`, `Dov’è`, `Quanto costa`, `Non capisco`, `Sì / Va bene`, `No, grazie`, and `Pago / Con la carta`. Refresher use is support evidence, not a penalty. The goal is repeated retrieval across scenes, not memorizing an isolated phrase list.

## English nouns inside Italian structures

An English noun is an acceptable placeholder when the Italian frame expresses the communicative move: `Vorrei un beach chair` demonstrates more useful control than a memorized noun without a request.

The system should:

1. interpret the likely noun only when context makes it safe;
2. resolve the practical outcome if essential details are clear;
3. model the Italian noun in the correction or NPC confirmation;
4. recur with that noun through the location and object rather than a detached flashcard; and
5. avoid marking the entire response wrong.

High-consequence ambiguity still requires clarification. “Ticket” at a kiosk with bus and ferry products cannot be silently resolved.

## Spanish interference

Spanish leakage is expected prior knowledge competing during retrieval, not carelessness. It can appear as vocabulary (`pero`), false friends, infinitive use, missing contractions, or Spanish-shaped word order.

Treatment rules:

- infer intent from Italian, Spanish, English, and encounter context together;
- acknowledge what worked before contrasting the languages;
- identify at most one high-value crossover at a time;
- use a compact contrast only when it will aid the immediate or recurring task;
- remember repeated interference patterns for future content selection, not NPC judgment; and
- never portray the NPC as knowing the player’s learning diagnosis unless that is an authored relationship fact.

Example: `No lavorare in Italia. Solo vacanza.` successfully communicates the broad intent. A useful model is `Non lavoro in Italia. Sono qui solo in vacanza.` The correction priority is the Italian negator and finite verb, not every omitted article or nuance.

## Correction priorities

After practical resolution, choose at most one issue using this order. Meaning-changing issues trigger the automatic correction; lower-priority naturalness improvements remain collapsed unless requested:

1. A misunderstanding that changes the outcome, price, time, location, or commitment.
2. A form needed to repair, refuse, clarify, or maintain the player’s boundary.
3. A repeated Spanish crossover likely to become habitual.
4. A high-frequency structure that would make the same move more reusable.
5. A naturalness improvement suited to the player’s chosen tone.

Do not prioritize low-impact agreement, spelling, or article errors when intent is clear. Never stack corrections merely because the model can detect them. Feedback uses the product contract’s “understood / natural / one variation” format after practical resolution. Meaning-changing corrections appear automatically; naturalness-only suggestions remain collapsed unless requested.

## How support fades

Support is adapted at the level of a communicative move and listening condition.

- A successful no-transcript response leads to less prominent cues the next time, not immediate removal.
- Transcript-first success leads to another audio-first opportunity with comparable language.
- Repeated replay may trigger shorter audio chunks or a familiar-speaker recurrence before harder audio.
- Failed interpretation can restore a phrase cue or simpler repair turn without rewinding the world automatically.
- New nouns do not justify removing support for a familiar structure; new structure plus new acoustic difficulty should be avoided.
- The player may explicitly choose a higher-support day without breaking progress.

The player-facing summary describes growing independence (“You handled the changed departure time after one replay”), not a hidden deficit score.

## Evaluation without arbitrary scoring

The system stores evidence and generates qualitative summaries across comparable tasks:

- practical outcome and consequential accuracy;
- whether the essential audio detail was acted on correctly;
- replays, slow replay, transcript, translation fallback, or cue use;
- whether the response was spontaneous, mixed-language, imitated, or repaired;
- number and type of required NPC clarifications;
- communicative moves demonstrated;
- repeated interference or misunderstanding patterns;
- ability to transfer a move to a different character, noun, or complication;
- ability to end or extend interaction deliberately; and
- recovery after a changed circumstance.

Improvement is a pattern such as: “Across three request encounters, moved from transcript plus cue to one replay; handled an unavailable item by requesting an alternative.” It is not “requesting: 82%.” Admin evaluation may aggregate counts for testing, but player progress remains consequence- and evidence-based.

## Day 0 calibration

Day 0 is a low-stakes arrival vignette, not an exam. It samples:

- audio-only gist from one short line;
- recognition after replay and after transcript reveal;
- ability to type a minimum request or response;
- comfort with mixed language;
- one clarification and one refusal/exit;
- likely Spanish crossover; and
- the player’s chosen default support and audio comfort.

The calibration may set starting support prominence but does not lock content, label a proficiency level, or deny an episode. The player can revise accessibility and support preferences at any time.

## Risks and safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard | Evidence to monitor |
|---|---|---|
| Reading masks listening weakness | Audio-first presentation; distinguish transcript-assisted from audio-only evidence | Correct detail before versus after transcript reveal |
| Repeated errors become habitual | Detect patterns across encounters; prioritize one recurring high-value contrast | Same interference across three or more contexts |
| The player memorizes a scene | Vary speaker, noun, order, and complication while preserving the move | Transfer to a new context |
| Support fade feels punitive | Keep help available; fade prominence gradually; allow player override | Abandonment or repeated failure after support change |
| Model accepts incoherent language too generously | Require essential slots and confidence thresholds; bounded clarification | False-positive outcomes in evaluator scenarios |
| Model rejects useful fragments | Define acceptable minimal intents and examples per encounter | False-negative outcomes for `Due notti`, `Solo questo`, etc. |
| Correction overwhelms the life objective | One-issue limit; defer until resolution; automatically show only meaning-changing issues and collapse naturalness feedback | Time spent in feedback and player dismissals |
| Spanish knowledge is stigmatized | Frame crossover as useful evidence and contrast, never total failure | Tone review and learner interviews |
| Familiarity becomes forced sociability | Separate required transaction from optional opening; honor immediate exit | Rate and success of declined conversations |

## Assumptions and unresolved validation

**Assumptions:** typed free-form responses can provide useful evidence without player speech; transcript concealment is acceptable when replay remains immediate; ten minutes allows one core objective plus at most one optional continuation.

**Needs validation:** the learner’s tolerance for realistic speech rate, how often meaning-changing automatic corrections feel welcome, and whether the chosen consequence intensity remains believable without making a short episode frustrating.
